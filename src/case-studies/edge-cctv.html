Case study · Edge computer vision

Cameras that count,
video that never leaves.

A coffee chain in Saudi Arabia already had CCTV in every store, and no answers from it. This turns those existing cameras into a live operations layer: footfall, dwell, queue waits, after-hours activity, PPE. Every frame is processed on a box sitting in the store, because under Saudi PDPL, blurring a face is not anonymization.

01The problem

Every store in the chain already had cameras. They recorded, and that was all they did. The questions the operations team actually had were simple ones: how many people came in this morning, how long did the queue get at noon, was anyone in the store after closing, is the team wearing what they are supposed to be wearing. Answering any of them meant someone scrubbing footage, so nobody answered them.

The normal way to build this is to stream the cameras to the cloud and run the models there. That option was closed before the first line of code.

02The constraint that shaped everything

Saudi PDPL treats video of identifiable people as personal data, and blurring faces does not count as true anonymization: a blur is a transformation, not a guarantee, and the original is still the thing you transmitted. So the raw frames could not go anywhere. Not to a cloud GPU, not to a regional endpoint, not into a bucket for later.

That single fact inverts the usual architecture. Instead of moving the video to the compute, the compute moves into the store, and the only thing allowed out is the conclusion.

"Blurring a face is not anonymization. Once that is true, the video cannot leave the building, and every other decision follows from there."

03The pipeline

1
Ingest the cameras that are already there
RTSP straight off the existing CCTV, decoded on the Jetson dedicated hardware decoder so the GPU is left free for inference. Nothing new goes into the ceiling, which is most of why a chain will actually say yes to a rollout.
2
Detect with a model the product can legally ship
The detector was moved to RF-DETR, which is Apache-2.0. The popular alternatives carry copyleft terms that follow you into a commercial product, and a licensing problem discovered after deployment is not one you can patch. Here the license was a design input, not paperwork.
3
Track, because detections alone say nothing
NvDCF multi-object tracking gives each person a stable identity across frames. A detection tells you a person is in frame; a track tells you it is the same person, moving, which is the only thing behaviour can be built on.
4
Turn tracks into events with a config-driven rule engine
Zones and lines are defined per store, and the rules sitting on them produce footfall, dwell, queue wait, after-hours and PPE events. It is config rather than code, so onboarding the next store is a file, not a release.
5
Send the conclusion, never the footage
Only structured events and small image crops leave the premises, into an isolated per-client cloud. The raw video stays on hardware the customer owns, which is what makes the system compliant rather than merely careful.

04The bug that was holding up three features

Counting looked finished long before it was right. The first implementation asked, every frame, whether a person was near the counting line. That is the intuitive version, and it is wrong in both directions: someone standing near the line gets counted again and again, and someone walking briskly can be on one side in one frame and past it in the next, never registering as near anything.

The fix was to stop looking at frames and start looking at the track. Take the segment between a track previous position and its current one, and test whether that segment intersects the line. One crossing per person, with a direction, and standing still produces nothing at all. Three of the analytics features sat on top of that single primitive, so a geometry correction in one place unblocked all three at once.

05Where it stands

The platform is at pilot stage, running on customer hardware. There are no accuracy or throughput numbers on this page on purpose: they have not been measured yet, and a number invented for a portfolio is worth less than admitting it does not exist. When the pilot produces real ones, they will go here.
